## Develop MVP Casing, test internal noise dampening - Oct. 31

We continued to refine our MVP based on our third prototype and the issues we found from it.

One big issue with our device was its ability to properly narrow a sound to one direction; it often doesn't seem very confident with a direction unless given a very clear sound from very close to the device. Aryan, Martin, and Adewola attempted to improve on this by stuffing the interior of the prototype with fabric to dampen any noise that may be reverberating within the device. They tested this approach, and it seemed to help.

At the same time, I worked on revising the casing design for the final MVP. I shrunk the size slightly to make the device a little bit more compact (though making it watch-size is not feasible within our time/financial restrictions). I also altered the cones around the sound sensors to allow the sound sensors to extend halfway into the cone, and I filled in a slight gap that was present between the cone and the side of the main cylinder. Some small tweaks were made in addition to this.

## Validation Testing (Day 1) - Nov. 6

We began validation testing on Friday. Our batch of participants included Prof. Fu, TA Roy, and a couple of friends. We used noise cancelling headphones to prevent the participants from hearing the external environment, and tasked them with identifying the direction of a speaker using only prompts from the device. Martin logged responses/success rate, and the last two participants were given a brief survey to evaluate their experience.

After this, we discussed amongst ourselves and with Dr. Fu the improvements we could make to our procedure to improve data collection and consistency. For example, in future testing, we will use a constant sound for each direction, rather than our voices, because each of us have different qualities to our voices which may impact device response.
